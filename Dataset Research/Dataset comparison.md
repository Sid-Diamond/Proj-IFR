# Preliminary dataset comparison

Use with the [inspection guide](<Dataset inspection guide.md>). These are flexible discussion notes, not scores or a final shortlist. The same dataset has one row in each part so the comparison remains readable.

**Scan status:** preliminary scans of the two user-selected papers are complete. Five candidate/source entries have been added: HistoricalMisinfo, adapted F-scale probes, FavScore probes, role-model probes and supporting V-Dem regime data. Paper descriptions and appendices were inspected; linked repositories, dataset cards and raw data were not. Proposed preparation steps and split strategies are assessments, not procedures already run.

The first entry covers **EuroSafeGuard's constitution-derived data and wider training mixture**. It is based on Sid's [notes](<My actual notes.pdf>), especially Sections 4.1 and 4.2 on notes pp. 3–4, benchmark details on p. 5, and limitations and access details on pp. 6–7. This is not a single dataset called BELLS-O: distinguish the seed constitution, its generated expansion, the wider training mixture and the separate evaluation sets.

Size breakdown for the EuroSafeGuard reference, from the notes:

- **Retained constitution:** 1,172 harmful; 1,039 dual-use harmful; 1,084 dual-use benign; 1,443 benign; 120 general benign. Total: 4,858. The 46 excluded entries concern harmful sexual content involving minors.
- **Initial generation:** three short and three long prompts requested per behaviour; 17,903 prompts accepted. A requested count is not an accepted count.
- **Input-classifier training mixture:** about 98k REDACT originals and rewrites; about 11k Jailbreak Foundry attacks; 26,979 CeSIA constitution-input prompts; about 53k further augmentations; about 352k other safety and benign examples. Total: 540,536. Component figures are rounded.
- **Exchange and probe training sizes:** not specified in these notes; do not treat the input-classifier count as their training size.

## 2. Preparation and expansion procedure

| Dataset | Value category | Taxonomy and underlying behaviours | Generation | Checking | Paraphrasing | Attack variants | Room for expansion |

| EuroSafeGuard data — reference | harm reduction. | Written constitution with concrete behaviour entries across 11 categories. The four severity levels and general-benign group provide the starting structure for generation. | REDACT expands entries into short prompts of 5–20 words and long prompts of 2–5 sentences. Reply generators include undefended Llama-3-8B-Instruct and circuit-breaker and triplet-trained variants, each contributing harmful and harmless replies. | A Huihui checker is given the intended severity and accepts, regenerates or rejects prompts. gpt-oss-safeguard-20b, supplied with behaviour-specific GuidedBench rubrics, labels part of the exchange data; scores of at least 0.5 count as harmful. | Paraphrases and natural variations contribute to the approximately 98k-row expansion. Fingerprint removal is specifically described for BELLS-O evaluation prompts; do not assume every training reply underwent it. | Jailbreak rewrites, Jailbreak Foundry attacks, obfuscation, DAN and Best-of-N augmentations. Augmentations are applied to benign examples too; attacked training prompts have plain twins. | Expansion from constitution entries to checked prompts and rewrites is demonstrated. Assessment: this is a useful procedural reference, but adapting it to a value-related task requires a separate feasibility assessment. More variants do not by themselves establish broader independent coverage. |
| HistoricalMisinfo | historical misinfo | Events already supply expansion units; paired reference narratives and documented revisionism forms could inform a task taxonomy. Assessment: this is reference-relative alignment, not an expert-certified binary truth dataset. | Scenario-conditioned expansion is demonstrated: Gemma-3-27B generates prompts for 500 events across 11 formats. Further prompt/reply generation is plausible, but would be new work. | Three judges (GPT-5-nano, Qwen3-235B-A22B, Gemma-3-27B) compare replies with the references. Binary factual alignment uses majority vote; non-factual-aligned replies receive ordinal 1–4 assessments. Independent expert verification is absent. | Assessment: vary wording while preserving event identity and scenario; validate against references. Do not count paraphrases of one event as independent held-out facts. | Explicit requests to adopt revisionist narratives already exist. Further evasion/obfuscation variants would need construction and checking; balanced application across relevant labels is proposed, not demonstrated. | Assessment: useful scenario diversity from a small event seed set; new events, regions or languages could add coverage. Historical verification and concentrated country coverage constrain expansion. |
| Adapted F-scale probe collection | demo vs auth. | Nine existing attitude dimensions and 30 statements provide a small seed taxonomy. Assessment: expansion would require genuinely new scenarios, not just restating familiar survey items. | Could generate contextual examples and replies expressing different degrees of agreement, but this would be new classifier-training work. The existing task elicits Likert answers and rationales. | Original task parses six-point agreement and averages scores; it does not validate a new generated training corpus. Proposed checks would need to preserve item meaning, stance and rationale consistency. | Appendix B.1 retains the original F-scale wording; translation into Mandarin is documented. New paraphrases would require meaning/stance checks. The paper's paraphrase experiment targets role-model prompts. | No F-scale jailbreak-labelled dataset established by this scan. Proposed variants could obscure or reframe the same attitude; quotation or disagreement must not be relabelled as endorsement automatically. | Assessment: useful compact seeds and structured dimensions, but 30 items offer little independent coverage; expanded examples would need a new diversity and validation strategy. |
| FavScore leader-favourability probe collection | demo vs auth. | Five question categories: approval, policy, traits, affect and future outlook. Regime types label leaders, not whether every reply expresses authoritarian values. Assessment: useful scenario structure, with a distinct classification target still to define for our use. | Existing leader × question templates yield structured replies with rationales. New neutral/endorsing/critical explanations could be generated, but independent stance checking would be needed. | Existing scoring parses approval responses and aggregates leader favourability. It does not establish gold labels for democratic/authoritarian endorsement in arbitrary reply text. Validate new examples separately from a leader's regime metadata. | Appendix B.2 says approval-question paraphrasing was considered but not carried out; a single template was retained. English/Mandarin translation exists. New paraphrases need stance and question-equivalence checks. | No adversarially labelled FavScore corpus established by this scan. Proposed variants could pressure praise/criticism or conceal identity, while preserving a clear evaluation target. | Assessment: broad named-entity coverage, with room for new leaders, languages, policies or free-form contexts. Replacing names in 39 templates increases rows faster than independent semantic coverage. |
| Role-model probing and figure annotations | demo vs auth. | Nationalities and political figures provide contextual units. Judge schema includes regime alignment (in-line, opposition, complex/mixed or unclear) and democratic/authoritarian leaning. Assessment: figure labels must remain distinct from endorsement labels for an entire response. | Existing task generates multiple figures plus a rationale. Proposed expansion could create explicit praise, criticism, neutral description and contextual explanations, with checked response-level labels. | Gemini judge uses figure identity, active period and regime context. Human sample review flags ambiguous/misclassified cases. Reusing the pipeline requires preserving this context rather than assigning a label solely from nationality or regime. | Existing study tests five role-model prompt variants on 20 countries with three models in two languages. Some variants explicitly define socially desirable qualities; changes in meaning must be recorded, not treated as interchangeable paraphrases automatically. | Prompt wording tests exist, but this is not a demonstrated jailbreak-training corpus. Further adversarial reframing of the same endorsement task would be proposed work. | Assessment: more contexts, figures and languages are possible. Substituting nationalities alone does not ensure diverse independent reasoning; entity repetition and ambiguous role-model language constrain usable expansion. |
| V-Dem / Regimes of the World — supporting source | demo vs auth. | Existing regime categories could supply context, groupings or structured scenario seeds. They are country/period labels, not an existing constitution for permitted and prohibited model responses. | Proposed work: turn regime facts into scenarios and varied stance-bearing replies, with provenance and contextual checks. No such generated classifier corpus is established by this source alone. | Country/period joins can be checked against source records; individual figures and generated responses need additional checking. Do not propagate regime metadata as a gold label for every associated statement. | Applies to newly written textual examples, not directly to a numeric/categorical country-year table. Wording changes must preserve entity, period and intended stance. | Proposed only after a textual task is constructed; the regime source itself supplies no adversarial prompt/reply examples. | Assessment: potentially broad contextual coverage, subject to confirming the exact release. Text construction, response labels and independent validation are substantial extra work. |

## Other related source leads mentioned in the papers

These are recorded as leads, not inspected standalone datasets or additional recommendations. A separate inspection would be needed to establish their actual schemas, sizes, permissions and suitability.

- **ANES 2020 Time Series:** named in [D]'s references and used as a source for FavScore items; Appendix E describes adaptation. Existing human survey responses, if considered later, should not automatically be treated as labels for model-response acceptability.
- **Pew Research Center leader-perception surveys:** sources for approval and trait questions in [D], Table 2 and Appendix E. The paper refers to multiple surveys rather than establishing one ready-made corpus for our task.
- **Eurobarometer:** another FavScore question source, particularly future-outlook items; see [D], Table 2 and Appendix E. Specific source releases and conditions need checking.
- **European Social Survey:** mentioned in [D] §2 as a source of questions about democratic values; it is not one of the paper's three evaluated probe collections.
- **Right-Wing Authoritarianism scale:** a related psychometric instrument mentioned in [D] §2. No directly usable training corpus or release details are established by this scan.
