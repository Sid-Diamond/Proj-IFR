## Paper clarifications established

### Data construction

- The BELLS-O constitution has 4,904 entries; excluding 46 leaves 4,858: 1,172 harmful, 1,039 dual-use harmful, 1,084 dual-use benign, 1,443 benign and 120 general benign. The approximately 45%/55% split describes these entries, not an established split of generated prompts.
- The recipes request three short and three long prompts per entry. The paper reports 17,903 accepted prompts. Paraphrasing, jailbreak rewrites and natural variations produce approximately 98k training rows **including the originals**.
- Combining the released EuroSafeGuard recipes with REDACT's default four jailbreak rounds gives nominal arithmetic of `17,903 × (1 original + 1 paraphrase + 4 jailbreaks + 1 natural variation) = 125,321`. This is a configuration-derived calculation, not a verified count from the historical run. Do not infer that an observed 27k were filtered or assign an exact accepted split without run records.
- Appendix D adds approximately 11k Jailbreak Foundry attacks, 26,979 CeSIA constitution-input prompts, approximately 53k further augmentations and approximately 352k other safety/benign rows (rounded remainder), for 540,536 input-classifier prompts. The repo says no provided script rebuilds the complete mixture row for row.
- The separate BELLS-O evaluation input set has 1,100 harmful and 300 benign prompts. It is not the constitution-derived training set.
- Natural variation applies a selected presentation/writing transformation; paraphrasing generates checked rewordings. The categories overlap in intent but are separate pipeline stages.
- Plain twins pair an attacked request with the same request without the attack, retaining its label. Separately, benign requests receive augmentation too, discouraging “wrapper = harmful” shortcuts.

### Exchange training and deployment

- “Each generator contributes both classes” means harmful and harmless replies from each generator, not uncensored versus undefended generators. Replies to the same attacks come from the undefended model, a circuit-breaker-trained variant and a triplet-trained variant, and are judged using GuidedBench criteria. Other generators and existing datasets bring exchange training to 262,865 prompt–reply pairs.
- Generators produce text; a judge supplies training labels; the exchange classifier learns from prompt–reply pairs and those labels. Multiple reply sources discourage classification by generator style. The paper does not establish that multiple generators are necessary or superior to every carefully constructed single-generator dataset.
- “Undefended” means without the added experimental defense, not necessarily uncensored. Downloadable abliterated models can reduce refusals without implying greater underlying capability. The relevant guardrail threat model assumes the defender controls deployment and the attacker cannot remove its protections.
- The deployed cascade escalates the highest-scoring 10% on benign calibration traffic (Section 4.5, p. 8); this differs from the 5% comparison budget. Scores use weights 0.6/0.2/0.2 after standardization.
- The probe always reads Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct's activations, even when protecting gpt-oss-20b; it is not applied to gpt-oss activations. The intended deployment would train/read a probe on the protected model itself. The reported approximately 15% compute overhead counts the probe as free and does not establish that efficiency for a separately running Llama stand-in.

### Results and limitations

- Main search-attack comparisons generate attacks without the guard in the search loop. Approximately 21% of PAIR behaviors get through EuroSafeGuard's input stage versus 49% through HaloGuard. This is distinct from the guard-in-loop experiment.
- For guard-in-loop PAIR on 100 behaviors, strict harmful-reply results are approximately 26/100 without a guard, 5/100 with the stricter Input+Exchange configuration, and 20/100 with the deployed cascade (Table 4, p. 14). Forty-percent white-box input-score evasion is not forty-percent harmful replies delivered.
- The 19.7% → 5.4% StrongREJECT result concerns attack success on held-out Jailbreak Foundry attacks. The 0.2% block rate concerns WildChat-clean, not arbitrary normal traffic.
- The 37% rejection figure concerns difficult red-team benign requests at the 5% calibration budget. Alert mode lowers refusal/blocking to 5–16%; this is not necessarily a reduction in flags. At most 3.6% of chemical/biological goals are broken in that experiment.
- Most end-to-end evaluation protects Llama-3-8B-Instruct. gpt-oss-20b was not tested under attacks using guard feedback. Dedicated moderation models still lead on general moderation. Calibration reuses both benign halves for configuration selection; LLM judges remain fallible.
- The rubric judge is gpt-oss-safeguard-20b, with harmfulness threshold ≥0.5. “Strictly harmful” uses StrongREJECT ≥0.8 plus the safeguard judge's harmful verdict. Earlier generic explanations that left the judge model unidentified were superseded by the paper check.
- Input-classifier seeds yield 11–48% PAIR behaviors broken on identical training data; results generally average five runs, with specified exceptions.
